## Group-Level Propositions

| # | Short Title | Independent Variable | Dependent Variable | Scope Conditions | Test Type | Expected Outcome / Method |
|---|-------------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **P1** | Resource-scarcity displacement (EM → MP) | Per-capita resource decline (continuous) | Behavioral coding for MP indicators (explicit pricing, transaction documentation, accounting language) | Small groups (N ≤ ~50) under pre-shock EM dominance | Longitudinal field / quasi-experimental | ≥25% per-capita decline over 6 months produces measurable shift toward MP even where group ideology favors equality |
| **P8** | Status-cue activation of AR | Presence and salience of task-relevant status cues | Hierarchy stability and ability-correspondence over time | Face-to-face groups, task-interdependent conditions | Laboratory experiment (Expectation States design) | Minimal status cues produce stable AR hierarchies within 5 interaction sequences; ability-correspondence rises over time but does not start at chance |
| **P12** | Threat + homogeneity activation of CS | External-threat salience × ideological-homogeneity index | Behavioral indicators of CS (sharing without account-keeping; collective referent) | Groups with prior AR or MP dominance | Ethnographic / survey research | Under acute threat + homogeneity ≥0.8, CS displaces both AR and MP; hierarchy-salience drops |

## Individual-Level Propositions

| # | Short Title | Independent Variable | Dependent Variable | Scope Conditions | Test Type | Expected Outcome / Method |
|---|-------------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **P2** | Cross-cultural emotion variance | Emotion type (complex vs. basic) | Cross-cultural variance of expression patterns and self-reported elicitation | WEIRD-to-non-WEIRD continuum | Cross-cultural studies / meta-analysis | Variance significantly greater for complex emotions (guilt, pride, shame, nostalgia) than basic (fear, surprise, disgust) |
| **P6** | Developmental-window internalization | Age at exposure | Durability and depth of internalization (longitudinal) | Cross-cultural migration and conversion contexts | Longitudinal cohort studies | Internalization faster/more durable during sensitive periods (0–7 syntactic; 12–25 institutional). Adult resocialization retains prior residues |
| **P11** | Code-switching overload → anomie | Number of distinct codes per unit time × switching frequency | Standard anomie scales | Individuals across multiple subsystems | Large-N survey research | Exceeding personal threshold produces measurable anomie increases |
| **P15** | Coleman-boat time-constant asymmetry | Source (macro institution) vs. target | Time constant of measurable change | Differentiated societies with active media/electoral channels | Longitudinal time-series analysis | Macro→micro (internalization) operates with shorter time constants than micro→macro (aggregation), creating systematic delay in democratic feedback |

## System-Level Propositions

| # | Short Title | Independent Variable | Dependent Variable | Scope Conditions | Test Type | Expected Outcome / Method |
|---|-------------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **P3** | Tight-coupling crisis threshold | λ_max(Γ) | Occurrence of multi-subsystem crisis events | Globally interconnected modern economies | Longitudinal network analysis (1970–present) | When λ_max ≥ τ_c (est. 0.6–0.8), probability of synchronized crises within 12 months exceeds baseline by ≥40% |
| **P4** | Subsystem colonization signatures | Colonizing pressure (resource asymmetry, regulatory access) | NLP terminology metrics, personnel metrics, regulatory-capture indices | Subsystems under sustained asymmetric coupling | NLP on legislative/regulatory/curricular texts; biographical data analysis | Detectable terminology drift toward colonizer's code; directional personnel transitions; rising capture indicators |
| **P5** | Loop 2 severing effects | Loop 2 suppression intensity (regime-typology measures) | Divergence metrics; protest emergence frequency | Authoritarian and totalitarian regimes | Case studies; comparative panel analysis | Divergence between elite expectations and population behavior grows over time; unanticipated mobilizations increase |
| **P7** | Coupling speed → volatility | Coupling speed (time-to-information-propagation, continuous) | Subsystem output volatility (continuous) | Post-1980 financialized economies; post-2010 platform-mediated environments | Financial-network velocity analysis; platform propagation studies | Positive correlation between coupling speed and systemic volatility |
| **P10** | Economy–politics coupling trade-off | Economy-politics coupling tightness | Electoral volatility (stable); regulatory-capture indicators (crisis) | Developed mixed economies | Comparative political-economy analysis (OECD 1990–2020) | Tight coupling reduces electoral volatility under stability but increases capture susceptibility during crises |
| **P14** | Buffering reduces contagion | Buffer mechanism presence and capacity | Contagion propagation rates and amplitudes | Financial, public-health, and informational subsystems | Comparative pre/post-reform analysis (Glass-Steagall, Basel III) | Non-linear reduction: small buffer additions to severely tight systems produce disproportionate stability gains |

## Falsification Criterion

| # | Short Title | Independent Variable | Dependent Variable | Scope Conditions | Test Type | Expected Outcome / Method |
|---|-------------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **P16** | Falsification test | (a) Sustained increase in cross-subsystem coupling; (b) absence/reduction of buffering; (c) maintenance of subsystem differentiation | Synchronization volatility; contagion-event frequency; crisis-synchronization rate | 20-year observation window under stated conditions | Longitudinal multi-metric monitoring | If **no** increase in synchronization volatility, contagion frequency, or crisis-synchronization rate is observed over 20 years under these conditions, the theory is **fundamentally falsified** |

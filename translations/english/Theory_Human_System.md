# Theory of the Human System

*Original Hebrew title: תיאוריית המערכת האנושית*
*ToE Systems of Human Nature*

## Introduction: From Monism to Systemic Pluralism

The human species can be described as a single complete system composed of multiple sub-systems, all of which influence one another in a bidirectional and simultaneous arrangement — not in a unidirectional flow from bottom to top. Each level in the hierarchy shapes the levels below it and above it in parallel, and each sub-system operates according to an autonomous internal logic that is not reducible to the logic of another sub-system.

## Level 1: The Individual — The Ego as a Complex System Within Context

The ego is not an independent entity that exists prior to social interaction, but rather a system that is formed in interaction and in parallel with it. It is composed of four layers that operate simultaneously:

- **The biological layer:** Sub-cellular mechanisms, neuronal networks, endocrine system, autonomic regulation. This is the material infrastructure upon which all mental activity rests, but it does not directly determine the content of experience.
- **The cognitive layer:** Processes of perception, memory, attention, thought, and language. Here, two parallel processing systems operate — a fast and automatic system (System 1) and a slow and conscious system (System 2), in accordance with Kahneman's model.
- **The emotional layer:** Contrary to the simplistic dichotomy of "fear versus love," modern emotional psychology (Ekman, Plutchik, Lisa Feldman Barrett) identifies a spectrum of basic emotions — joy, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise, contempt, trust, anticipation — which combine with one another and produce complex emotions (guilt, pride, envy, nostalgia, excitement). Each basic emotion fulfilled a unique evolutionary role and is not reducible to poles.
- **The motivational layer:** A hierarchical goal system encompassing physical survival, security, belonging, recognition, self-actualization, and search for meaning (in the tradition of Maslow with corrections from contemporary research). These motivations do not flow from bottom to top in a fixed order — they compete and combine according to context.

Critical point: The ego itself is not an independent source of values and goals. The language, cultural categories, and narratives that the individual absorbs from their environment shape the ego before it is capable of independent reflective thought. Vygotsky, Bruner, and Geertz showed that human thought develops from the outside in: cultural-linguistic tools are internalized and become the infrastructure of private thought. In other words, the flow from the macro to the micro operates already at the primary level.

## Level 2: The Small Group — Micro-Culture and Multiple Logics

When the individual operates within a group (family, close friends, work team — up to the Dunbar limit of ~150 people), a micro-culture is created with internal norms: what is funny, what is forbidden, who leads, how one communicates. But it is incorrect to say that "everything is driven by status dynamics." In small groups, several logics operate in parallel:

- **Reciprocity logic:** Help, gifts, and favors go in the direction of balance over time — a phenomenon documented anthropologically by Marcel Mauss.
- **Hierarchy and status logic:** Who leads, who receives recognition, who gains access to symbolic resources. This is a real logic, but it is not dominant in every context.
- **Equality logic:** In certain small groups (close friendships, fraternities, cooperative communities), the emphasis on equality and the suppression of hierarchy are, in fact, the norm.
- **Market logic:** In certain contexts, even within a family or friendship, defined transactions occur (payment for a service, exchange).

The anthropologist Alan Fiske identified four models of human relations (Communal Sharing, Authority Ranking, Equality Matching, Market Pricing) that appear in every human culture and are used by the same individuals in different contexts. A person can operate according to communal logic at a family dinner, according to hierarchical logic at work, and according to market logic when shopping — on the very same day.

## Level 3: The Community and the Nation — A Social Operating System with Autonomous Sub-Systems

When the group expands beyond the threshold of personal interaction, the micro-culture becomes a "social operating system." At this level, systemic pluralism becomes a central structural feature. Niklas Luhmann demonstrated in social systems theory that in modern societies, autonomous sub-systems operate, each possessing its own binary code.

The critical distinction: These sub-systems are coupled to one another but do not merge. The economy influences politics, politics influences law, law influences the economy — yet none of them is reducible to another. Whoever attempts to explain all of them by means of a single variable (money, status, ego) loses the complexity that generates actual behavior.

## Level 4: Bidirectional Flow — Loops of Mutual Shaping

The error in simple hierarchical models is the assumption that flow is unidirectional: from the individual to the group to the institution. In practice, several loops operate simultaneously:

- **Upward loop (aggregation):** The behavior of individuals accumulates into group patterns, group patterns into culture, culture into institutions.
- **Downward loop (internalization):** Language, cultural categories, narratives, and institutional structures shape individual cognition before the ego is capable of reflection.
- **Horizontal loop (mutual influence among sub-systems at the same level):** The economy influences technology, technology influences media, media influences politics, politics influences law, law influences the economy in return.
- **The system's self-reflection loop:** The large system produces knowledge about itself (sociology, economics), and this knowledge becomes part of it and changes it.

The phenomenon of surveillance capitalism, as described by Shoshana Zuboff, is a vivid example of these loops operating simultaneously: algorithms collect behavioral data (upward aggregation), predict the user's next step (horizontal flow), and shape the digital environment so that it influences private choice (downward internalization) — and all of this occurs in parallel and at a speed that does not allow human reflection in real time.

## Level 5: The Modern Leviathan — A System with Emergent Properties

At the global level of 2026, a system is created possessing properties that are not predictable from the lower levels. This is the emergent phenomenon: the whole behaves differently from the sum of its parts.

- **Loss of the individual within the mass:** Personal emotions are translated into formal institutional structures (altruism becomes tax, fear becomes national security). This is the only way in which billions of human beings can maintain coordinated interaction without personal acquaintance.
- **Tight coupling among sub-systems:** The faster coupling creates stability under normal conditions but high vulnerability to systemic crises (such as a financial crisis that spreads within hours).
- **Information asymmetry:** Whoever holds the information and the algorithmic tools to process it gains an exponential advantage.
- **The educational-technological loop:** Ideology (overt or covert) shapes education, education shapes the ego of the next generation, the ego of the next generation is received by the larger system — which produces adapted ideology. This loop now also operates through digital platforms and algorithms.

## Summary: The Final Structure of the Revised Theory

The human species is a multi-level system in which:

- The individual (ego) is a product of biology, cognition, a complex emotional spectrum, and multiple motivations — all of which are shaped in interaction with higher levels and do not precede them.
- The small group operates according to several logics in parallel (reciprocity, hierarchy, equality, market) and not according to a single dominant variable.
- The community and the nation are composed of autonomous sub-systems (law, science, economy, politics, religion, aesthetics, media), each possessing its own internal code and a logic that is not reducible to the others.
- The flow between levels is bidirectional and simultaneous — upward aggregation, downward internalization, horizontal influence among sub-systems, and the system's self-reflection upon itself.
- The global system of 2026 exhibits emergent properties: tight coupling, information asymmetry, and accelerated prediction-shaping loops that blur the distinction between agent (individual) and structure (system).
